# The best spreadsheet has a notes column.

A model often outlives the conversation that created it. Someone returns to a workbook months later and sees a growth rate of 4.5%, a margin of 18%, and a discount rate copied across several tabs. The formulas may still work while the reasons behind the inputs have disappeared.

## Document the decision, not just the number

A useful assumption record includes a definition, unit, source, observation date, and a sentence explaining why the input belongs in this model. If a number is hypothetical, label it that way. If it is a management target rather than an observed result, preserve the distinction.

| Input | Example note |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue growth | Hypothetical scenario; not a forecast |
| Operating margin | Held constant to isolate the effect of sales growth |
| Discount rate | Sensitivity range, not a measured cost of capital |

## Give uncertainty a place to live

Notes also make it easier to disagree productively. A reviewer can challenge an assumption without first reverse-engineering the workbook. The author can distinguish a deliberate simplification from an accidental omission.

The goal is not to make every spreadsheet longer. It is to keep the reasoning near the result. A short note written when a decision is fresh can save an hour of confident guessing later.
